Field goals, punts and extra points are called from the **Special Teams** formation on the play-call screen. Fakes are there too.

## Kicking

Field goals, extra points and punts use the kick meter, as kickoffs do (chapter 5):

1. Press <Btn k="C" /> to start the meter.
2. Aim with left and right. If there is wind, aim to make up for it.
3. Press <Btn k="C" /> to kick. The higher the meter, the longer the kick.

A kicker's distance comes from his leg strength, and his ACCURACY rating sets how far a kick can drift off line (chapter 11).

## Defending a kick

On defense, the Special Teams formation has calls to return or rush a punt and to block a field goal. On a punt return, the deep man fields the ball; press a direction on the D-pad to control him. To block a kick, move your player into the kick's path and press <Btn k="C" /> to jump.

## Extra points and two-point tries

After a touchdown, kick the extra point or run a play from the 3-yard line for two points. If the defense recovers a fumble on the try, the team that scored kicks off <New />. In the original game, the wrong team kicked off.

<Edition only="2026">
**Overtime** <New />: every tied game goes to overtime, as in college football today. Each team gets a possession from the other team's 25-yard line; if the score is still tied, another overtime follows. From the 2nd overtime, a team that scores a touchdown must go for two (a kicked extra point counts 0). From the 3rd overtime, the teams take turns trying two-point plays from the 3. If the defense scores during the first possession, it wins. Press a button on the menu that opens overtime to start it.
</Edition>

<Edition only="1994">
In a season, playoff and title games can't end in a tie; they go to sudden-death overtime. Regular-season and Exhibition games can end tied, as in the original game.
</Edition>
