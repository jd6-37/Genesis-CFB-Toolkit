On defense you call the formation and the play the same way as on offense (chapter 6). The defensive formations are **4-4, 4-3, 3-4, Nickel** (five defensive backs), **Dime** (six), **Goal Line** and the special teams.

**SWAP MLBS** <New /> on the top row puts your second middle linebacker (MLB2) in for your starter, or back, the same way SWAP HBS works on offense. <Btn k="Z" /> <Six /> does it too.

**Shifts** <New /> <Six />: tap <Btn k="X" />, <Btn k="Z" /> or <Btn k="Y" /> to pick the line, the linebackers or the safeties. The group's name (Shift Line, Shift LBs or Shift Safeties) shows at the top right. Then press left or right to move the group one step that way (up to two steps). Press down or up to pinch or spread the line and linebackers, or to move the safeties up or back. Press the same button again to return the group to where the call lines them up. The players sidestep into place, facing the line.

When you hold <Btn k="X" />, each defender gets a marker: **R** rush, **Z** zone, **M** man, **S** spy.

After a defensive audible, your players walk to their new spots twice as fast as in the original game <New />, so they are less likely to be out of place at the snap.

The defender you control stands on a circle. Your audibles are set in the pregame or on the pause menu, as on offense (chapter 4).

## After the snap

The defense plays the call by itself: linemen rush, and defensive backs cover receivers. Take over whenever you want.

Every time a defender touches the ball carrier, the game makes a random tackle check: the defender's TACKLE against the carrier's POWER. If the check fails, the tackle is broken and the carrier keeps running until the next defender touches him. More in chapter 11, and in the Insights post [What each player rating does in the game](/insights/player-ratings).

## What the CPU does

From the Smart difficulty up, the CPU offense calls audibles against your formation (against Nickel or Dime, usually to a run play) and goes for it on 4th and short. The CPU also calls more plays to stop what has worked for you. When you flip a play, the CPU defense flips its alignment and assignments 9 times out of 10 <New />. CPU teams also swap their halfbacks and their middle linebackers when the starter gets tired <New />.
