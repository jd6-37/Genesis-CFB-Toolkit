# Dynasty

The Dynasty section (`#dynasty`) keeps a multi-season record of the 138-team 2026 Season Edition: the app owns the players, the game plays the games, and the battery save carries each season's results back to the app.

Code: `src/core/dynasty/` (no DOM) and `src/tools/dynasty/` (the pages). Tests: `npm run test:dynasty -- ELEVATED_2026_138_DYNASTY SAVE.srm` (v2.0.1-test9 or later; the test marks the save for its dynasty).

## The battery save

RetroArch's Genesis Plus GX core writes a 65,456-byte `.srm`: cartridge save RAM from $200000, one data byte per word on the odd addresses, so file offset = address − $200000. A file of the data bytes alone (half the size) is read too.

- $200001: signature 0x6F. $200003: 0 once a season exists.
- Season block at $200E01, 0x1330 data bytes (the game copies it with the routines at 0x05B452 and 0x05B48C):

| Offset | Size | What |
|---|---|---|
| +0 | 1 | 0x5A |
| +1 | 1 | index of the last regular-season week |
| +2 | 1 | number of weeks (regular season and postseason) |
| +3 | 1 | flags: bit 0 postseason, bit 1 Real 2026 schedule, bit 2 CFP 24 |
| +6 | word | number of games |
| +8 | words | first game of each week, and one past the last |
| +$34 | 24 | playoff seeds (team numbers, seed 1 first) |
| +$50 | 138 words | power ratings; the Top 25 is the teams in power order, ties by team number |
| +$164 | 138 | each team's rank at the end of last week |
| +$1EE | 18 | the user's teams, one bit per team |
| +$200 | 4 per game | visitor score, visitor, home score, home. A score of FF = not played, FE = HOLD (a user's game); a team of FF = to be decided |

- Season stats at $203951: 138 records of 0xB8 data bytes, one per team, kept per roster slot (season totals, not per game). Labels from the game's Season Stats screens and its category table at 0x0DFB08:

| Category | Offset | Slots | Fields (w = word, sw = signed word, b = byte) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Passing | 0 | 0 | att w, comp w, yds sw, int b, td b |
| Rushing | 8 | 0-3, 8 bytes each | att w, yds sw, long, short, fum, td |
| Receiving | 40 | 1-3, 9-14 | rec w, yds sw, long, drops, fum, td |
| Kicking | 112 | 15 | FGA, FGM, long, blocked, XPA, XPM |
| Punting | 118 | 31 | punts w, yds sw, long, inside 10 |
| Defense | 124 | 16-30, 4 bytes each | tackles w, sacks, int |

A record still all FF has never been cleared and reads as zeros. Passing and receiving add up across the league but not always per team (the game sometimes credits a catch without a completion).

Week names follow the game: with the Real 2026 schedule the first week is Week 0; after the regular season come the conference championships and then the playoff rounds (1, 2, 4 or 5 rounds for 2, 4, 12 or 24 teams).

## The dynasty

**Rosters: 64 per team** (user, 2026-11-12): the Active Roster (the 32 in the game) and a backup for each slot (`rosters.ts`). The 32 are the Rosters tool's CFB 27 conversion of the built-in ratings (what the ROM holds); the backups are the same conversion run again over the players left over, so each slot's backup plays its position, rated on the 32's scale (`rateAgainst` in `rosterCfb27.ts`: for each rating, the step of the converted player of the same role whose CFB 27 score is closest below his). The dynasty keeps only these players (about 8,500 at the start; K/P backups can be shared). This first season is the only use of the CFB 27 data (user, 2026-11-12): from then on the players' own Bill Walsh '95 ratings are the truth ("Ratings" below). In the first season the user can replace every team's backups with generated players (`bench.ts`, Manage page). Pages show all 64, clearly split into "Active Roster" and "Backups" (user, 2026-11-12); a backup's page shows his ratings if he played, ranked against the Active Roster players.

`Dynasty` (`model.ts`) holds the teams (school, nickname, city and uniform colors from the ROM), every player (name, position, class FR/SO/JR/SR/SR+, jersey, height, weight, hometown, OVR, his Bill Walsh '95 ratings, a skin tone 0-3 for the app's portraits, status) and the seasons. A season has its year, the conferences and their names from the ROM, the 32 roster slots of each team (which dynasty player the ROM has in each slot, so slot stats belong to players), each slot's Bill Walsh '95 ratings (the six 0-15 steps the game uses; at creation they are the Rosters tool's CFB 27 conversion, which for v2.0.0 is exactly what the ROM holds in all 4,416 slots) and the imported saves. Player pages show only these game ratings; a player who isn't in one of his team's 32 slots has none.

- Created from the ROM and the built-in CFB 27 ratings, with the Rosters tool's own CFB 27 conversion choosing each team's 32. A redshirted player starts one class up.
- The dynasty file is version 2 (Bill Walsh '95 ratings); version 1 dynasties (CFB 27 ratings, earlier test versions) can't be opened.

### Ratings

`ratings.ts` (user, 2026-11-12: "Why are you still using the cfb27 data?! That was only used to convert the first roster"). Every player keeps a number (0-100, one decimal) for Speed, Quickness, Power, Awareness, Hands, Range, Accuracy, Blocking and Tackling, and the game's Weight in pounds (the OL tweak's pounds are added on screen as before). Players also keep the related rating group's numbers for position changes: defensive linemen a Quickness, linebackers a Power (from the same conversion, rated as the other role).

- **Fine ratings** (`fine.ts`; Elevated 2026 v2.0.1-test13 on; user, 2026-11-12: "We've completed the fine-ratings mod … do whatever you need to do in the app to show those ratings, adjust progression, recruits, etc."; "I'm not worried about making old saves compatible"):
  - The game plays with a fine value per rating, 16 × step + offset. The step (0-15) is in the roster record; the offset (−8..+7) is in a table. Team Rosters shows ⌊fine × 100 / 240⌋, at most 99.
  - The app's number × 2.4 is the fine value (`fineOf`, 100 = 240). The record gets the nearest step and the table the rest (`splitFine`; before the mod a number counted as the step it had reached). Weight: pounds − 140 is the fine value.
  - The app shows the number the game shows (`shownOf`, `shownFine`): 0-99.
  - **First season:** the Active Roster's offsets are read from the ROM's 2026 table at 0x2E0000 when the record has the conversion's steps (all 4,416 slots on test14), so the app shows what Team Rosters shows. Backups aren't in the ROM: theirs are drawn evenly (a step's average stays the step). The app's numbers stop at 100, so the few offsets above step 15 are dropped (the game shows 99 either way), and an offset below step 0 counts as 0, as in the game. On a ROM without the mod, all offsets are drawn.
  - **Later seasons:** every slot's step and offset come from the player's number (`bwSlotOf`); `BwSlot.offs` holds the offsets. The season ROM gets them as a set in the dynasty area (see "The season ROM"). The game doesn't read that set yet (up to test14 the snap and Team Rosters use no offsets for a dynasty roster, so dynasty seasons play and show whole steps); handoff `claude/bw95-dynasty-fine-handoff.md`.
  - 30 CPU-only seasons with the fine model: the league stays at the 2026 level (median team best-22 OVR 79.4-80.8 every year, starters 76-78).
- **OVR** (`ovrOf`, `ovrAt`, `playerOvr`): the OVR formulas below on the shown numbers (from the fine values), at his position's role, or a slot's role for the depth chart.
- **Progress:** development raises the numbers and every point plays. The player page shows each rating's number, a 15-part meter filled to the fine value, and his rank among the league's players at the position.
- **Depth charts** (`depth.ts`): each slot goes to the player of its position with the best OVR at the slot's role, the Active Roster first, then the backups; then close positions (HB: FB, WR; DE: the other DE, DT; CB: FS, SS …), then anyone. A kicker can also punt when a team has no punter.
- **New players** (`makeRecruit`, `recruitTemplates`): modeled on a league player of the position at the star rating's place (5★ top 3%, 4★ next 17%, 3★ 35-80%, 2★ 5-35%, 1★ bottom 5%; walk-ons bottom 8%), younger by 7-13 OVR points (spread over the ratings his OVR counts, Speed at 40%), every rating ±3, to one decimal (not whole numbers or steps). Names and hometowns from the starting players. In a six-season run (2026-2032, random classes) the starters' average OVR stays at 75-78.

### Headshots

`headshot.ts`, `Headshot` in `ui.tsx` (user, 2026-11-12: "16-bit headshots on the player page"; the shaded style; "plain gray. maybe darker?"). The player page shows a 40 × 40 pixel portrait drawn in a canvas and scaled up with crisp pixels, with a gray (#ccc) border (user, 2026-11-12). The picture isn't stored: `lookOf` makes the look from the player on every page view. **Appearance editing** (user, 2026-11-12: "make the appearance attributes of a player editable anytime during the season … anything that doesn't affect the ROM"): `DPlayer.look` keeps only the parts the user changed (`LookEdit`: hair, hair color, facial hair, expression, eye black; `editLook` in edit.ts, also the skin tone); `lookOf` applies them over the generated look. Allowed any time, any player (also departed ones); "Back to his original look" clears it. Since the portrait is drawn live, saving just stores the choices: nothing is regenerated.
- **From the player:** his skin tone (the four tones), and his build from his position: wide neck and shoulders for OL and DL, medium for HB, FB, TE and LB, slim for the rest.
- **From his ID** (seeded, the same every time): hair style (short with a side part, buzz, fade, curls, afro, locs, flowing, twists, mohawk, bald) and hair color, facial hair (freshmen and sophomores get stubble at most), mouth (neutral, smile, smirk, serious with lowered brows, grin) and eye black (3 in 10).
- **Drawing:** the jersey in the team's recorded main color with a V collar in its second color (gray and white for a player without a team); light from the left with shaded right sides; ears set against the head's outermost columns; eyes looking straight ahead and the face mirrored about its center; long hair (flowing, locs) falls behind the head onto the shoulders. Background: a plain darker gray gradient (a logo behind the player was too busy).

### Editing

`edit.ts`, `EditPlayer.tsx` (user, 2026-11-12: "full capabilities like we have in the mod toolkit"; every team). In the offseason and before a season's first results are imported (`editLock`):
- **Player page:** Edit player: name, position, class, number, height, weight, hometown, potential, team (moves him), and his ratings (0-100; the form shows the number the game shows; Game weight in pounds). Release him (he leaves the dynasty).
- **Team page:** Add a player at a position (a generated walk-on, then edit him).
- **Manage, Players (CSV):** download every player (ID, Team, names, Position, Class, Jersey, Height, Weight, Hometown, Home State, OVR, Potential, Game Weight and the nine ratings); import an edited file: rows with an ID change that player (only what differs), rows without one add a player (Team and Position needed), a cleared Team releases him. The changes and any problems are listed first; nothing changes until Apply.
- In the preseason the season follows at once: teams whose players came or went get a new depth chart, the others keep their lineups with the new ratings; team OVRs move by the starters' change; the ROM must be downloaded again (roster version + 1). In the offseason the depth charts come at its end.
- Each import keeps the save's data bytes (base64). One import per number of completed weeks, the newest kept. An import with fewer results than the last is refused unless allowed; one whose games differ from the last import's is refused as another season. Teams the save marks as the user's are added.
- Everything shown (records, standings, ranks, leaders, records, champions, stories) is derived from the imports (`derive.ts`, `news.ts`), so the same saves always give the same pages.

### Team and player OVRs

- Team OFF and DEF are the ROM's own (what Game Setup shows), read when the dynasty is created.
- A player's OVR comes from his six Bill Walsh '95 ratings: OVR = base + scale × the weighted average of the ratings as the game shows them (0-100), by roster role, capped at 0-99 (`src/core/dynasty/ovr.ts`). Only ratings the game uses at that position count: Weight is mass, not quality; kickers' and punters' Range isn't used for kicks (their Quick is kick distance). Linemen's Block counts: it keeps them on their feet after hard hits. QB Speed is set to 15% (the QB runs in the game; CFB 27's Overall ignores it). The shares, base and scale were fitted to the CFB 27 Overall of the 4,416 starters of v2.0.0:

| Slots | OVR | Average miss | Within 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| QB | 57 + 0.42 × (Quickness 25%, Awareness 45%, Range 10%, Power 5%, Speed 15%) | 2.2 | 82% |
| HB1, HB2 | 48 + 0.51 × (Speed 35%, Quickness 30%, Awareness 15%, Hands 5%, Power 15%) | 1.3 | 96% |
| FB | 46 + 0.4 × (Speed 40%, Awareness 20%, Hands 15%, Power 25%) | 2.2 | 80% |
| SE, FL, WR1, WR2 | 52 + 0.5 × (Speed 15%, Quickness 25%, Awareness 25%, Hands 15%, Power 20%) | 1.4 | 95% |
| TE1, TE2 | 55 + 0.51 × (Speed 20%, Quickness 25%, Awareness 20%, Hands 25%, Power 10%) | 1.6 | 89% |
| LT, LG, C, RG, RT | 53 + 0.48 × (Power 30%, Blocking 30%, Speed 25%, Awareness 15%) | 1.2 | 98% |
| LDE, RDE | 48 + 0.52 × (Power 15%, Speed 35%, Awareness 20%, Tackling 30%) | 1.2 | 97% |
| DT1, DT2 | 56 + 0.38 × (Power 60%, Speed 10%, Awareness 15%, Tackling 15%) | 1.0 | 97% |

- All starters: average miss 1.4 from CFB 27, 94% within 3 (`test:dynasty` checks every role stays under 2.5).

### Team colors

The pages use each school's official primary and secondary colors (`src/core/dynasty/schoolColors.ts`, all 138 schools by the ROM's school name); a school missing there falls back to the ROM's team color (the jersey when that is near black or white). The ROM's colors are tuned for the Genesis palette and the uniforms, so they often read wrong on a page (LSU too blue, Iowa blue, Georgia too dark). Marks, meters and charts on white use the primary, or the secondary when the primary is too light (Iowa's gold → black). A game's split picture uses the second team's secondary when both primaries are close (Louisiana and USC reds).

### Storage and the dynasty file

IndexedDB database `genesis-cfb-dynasty`: store `dynasties` (the dynasty and when it changed) and store `art` (each team's logo, helmet and wordmark drawn from the user's ROM). The dynasty file is the dynasty as JSON with `app: "genesis-cfb-toolkit"`, `tool: "dynasty"`, `version: 1`; it never carries the art. A file from a newer version is refused.

### Lineups

`src/core/dynasty/lineup.ts` (user, 2026-11-12). A team has 64 spots: the 32 slots (the Active Roster) and each slot's backup, named QB2, HB3 and HB4 (behind HB1 and HB2), FB2, SE2, FL2, WR3, WR4, TE3, TE4, LT2 … RT2, LDE2, DT3, DT4, RDE2, LOLB2, MLB3, MLB4, ROLB2, LCB3, LCB4, RCB3, RCB4, SS2, FS3, FS4, K2, P2 (`backupLabel`: the family's size plus the slot's number). On a user team's page the Pos column is a menu, grouped by position with ★ on the Active Roster spots; choosing a spot swaps the player with the one there (who must be able to play the spot left; an Active Roster spot is never left empty).

- **Which spots:** any spot of his position (by `DPlayer.pos`, plus the kind of spot he already holds), and one related position: HB↔FB, FB↔TE, DE↔OLB, OLB↔MLB, K↔P. No CB/S or DE/DT (user: so nobody signs corners to play safety). A change to another position sets his `pos` (LE/RE, LOLB/ROLB by the spot) and his CFB 27-style OVR, and is recorded in `posChange` with the season: once a season, so a TE needs a season at FB before HB. In that season he can still move between the spots of both positions (back to his old one undoes the change).
- **Ratings:** he plays with his own numbers (ratings.ts), as the new slot's rating group uses them.
- **Team OVRs** move by the change in `starterOvr` of the team's starters (season one's are the ROM's own).
- **When:** user teams only, outside the offseason, before any imported save has a result (stats belong to slots). `DSeason.lineupEdited` shows a reminder to download the season's ROM again (cleared by the download). The next offseason's `depthChart` sets every lineup again from the players' positions.

## The season ROM

`seasonRom` (`offseason.ts`) writes a season's 32 per team (jersey numbers and game ratings, on the 2026 record of each slot) and the team OVRs into a copy of the user's ROM, checksums fixed.

- **Only ROMs with the dynasty support and Team Rosters' names** (Elevated 2026 Season Edition v2.0.1-test9 on, user 2026-11-12: the mark `BW95DYN` + version at `0x2B0000`, the 2026 names table `NAM1` at `0x2C0000` and the names view's row hook at `0x06AFC0`; `hasDynastyHooks` in `dynastyArea.ts`); older ROMs and saves aren't supported before the first public release (user, 2026-11-12: "things are changing fast and big"). Everything goes into the dynasty area `0x3E0000`-`0x3FFFFF`, which Season mode reads and Exhibition never does. Header: the ID block (`DYN1`, dynasty ID = FNV-1a of the dynasty's app ID, season number = season index + 1, roster version, export time in Unix seconds), format word 1, team count 138, then the addresses of the roster set (`0x3E0100`, 138 × 32 game records), the OVR table (`0x3E4600`, `OVR1` + OFF/DEF bytes), the sim words (`0x3E4800`, `simFromOvr`) and (+0x20) the names set (`0x3E5000`, `NAM1`: team count, a long per team, then per slot class byte 0-4, first name, NUL, last name, NUL; Team Rosters' names view in v2.0.1-test9 on; season one's set equals the ROM's own 2026 names table at `0x2C0000`). The ID block is written last. The area must be all `0xFF` or hold a valid header (`DYN1`, `0x0001008A` at +0x10); the 2026 data stays as it is. Season one's sets equal the ROM's own rosters and OVRs.
- **Roster version:** 1, then one more when the lineup or the schedule changes after the last download (`DSeason.rosterVersion`, lineup and schedule editors).
- **Fine-ratings offsets:** header +0x24 → `FRC1` + 138 × 32 × 3 bytes at 0x3FB000, six nibbles a slot in the roster record's order (−8..+7), the format of the ROM's 2026 table at 0x2E0000 (`putFineOffsets`, `fineAddress`). The names set may now run to 0x3FB000.
- **Schedule** (from the second season): header +0x28 → `SCH1` set at 0x3FF000 (see "Schedules"); 0xFFFFFFFF in the first season.
- **The save stamp:** New Season copies the ID block into the season block's +0x1330. `importSave` only takes stamped saves: another dynasty's or season's are refused (this also refuses last season's save in a new season), and an older roster version than the season's gives a warning (the season was started before the last lineup change). Exception: the first season's save without a stamp (started with the plain 2026 ROM, which has the same rosters as the app's first-season ROM) is taken with a warning (user's save, 2026-11-12); later seasons need the stamp.
- The Manage page has a Download button for the current season's ROM (the first season's too, so its save gets the stamp); the Offseason page has one for the next season; the lineup editor reminds the user to download again.

## Schedules

From the second season the app makes each season's schedule (`schedule.ts`; user, 2026-11-12: "Generate each year, and then the user can edit … Basically use same rules"). The first season plays the one chosen in the game (Real 2026 or Random).

- **Rules** (the season engine's Random schedule, `GEN`):
  - 12 weeks, and every team plays every week.
  - Each conference plays its conference games as circle-method rounds of a shuffled order. The number of games and the first week come from the ROM's CWN/CWS tables, read when the dynasty is created and kept in `DSeason.confWeeks`. Dynasties made before that use the released values.
  - Everyone else is paired at random: another conference first, then no repeats.
  - Home and away: rivalry games switch sites from last season. Otherwise the team with fewer home games hosts, evened out so every team has 5-7.
- **Protected rivalries** (`PROTECTED` in `rivalries.ts`, 45 pairs; user: "Alabama should always play Auburn (ideally, last week of season). Michigan should always play Ohio State, even though there are 18 teams in the Big Ten").
  - Inside a conference, the rounds are picked so they hold the conference's protected games.
  - 20 season finales go in week 12 (Iron Bowl, The Game, Egg Bowl, Clean, Old-Fashioned Hate, Army-Navy …). When a finale isn't there, its two teams leave their week-12 games, and those games move to a week where both teams are free.
  - Protected games across conferences go in a week where both teams are free.
  - Which pairs are protected, and which are finales, was Claude's pick.
  - Checked: every protected game on the schedule and every finale in week 12, over 30 schedules.
- **Editing** (`SchedulePage.tsx`, `#dynasty/schedule/<team>`, linked from a user team's page and the new-season ROM window; user: "We don't need to edit cpu teams, only the dynasty team(s)"; "I would like to be able to edit conference games too"):
  - Per week, the user can change his team's opponent, conference teams included (`setOpponent`). His old opponent then plays the new opponent's old opponent, so everyone still plays once a week.
  - He can move the game to another week (`moveGame`, two such swaps; choosing an opponent he already plays moves that game), or switch home and away (`flipHome`).
  - The page shows conference games, home games, rivalries, games played twice and protected rivalries no longer on the schedule.
  - "Make a new schedule" makes another one for everyone.
  - Locked once the season has an import. A change after the ROM download raises the roster version and asks for a new download (like a lineup change).
- **In the ROM:** header +0x28 points to the schedule set at 0x3FF000. The set is `SCH1`, weeks, then per week a count and visitor/home bytes, the format of the ROM's real 2026 table (see "The season ROM"). The game needs a change to play it (New Season reads it instead of making one); the handoff is `claude/bw95-dynasty-schedule-handoff.md` in the claude.ai project. Until then New Season ignores it.
- **Import:** a save whose regular-season games aren't the dynasty's schedule gets a warning. The results count as played.
- Before the season's first import, the pages show the dynasty's schedule (unplayed games).

## The front page

Layout (user, 2026-11-12; mobile first): the stories, then the Top 25 in the main column (from 600 px the top four as cards and the rest in two columns, three from 900 px; phones one list); the side column has the headlines, your teams, next week's big games and the Heisman watch; a band across the page below has the playoff picture, the national leaders (top three in passing, rushing and receiving yards) and your conference's standings (one table per conference of your teams). In the offseason the side column shows your commitments and the recruiting rankings (commitments by class points), then the top classes; before a season's first game, the classes it signed. `src/tools/dynasty/HomeSide.tsx`, `src/core/dynasty/outlook.ts`:

- **Big games** (`bigGames`): both teams ranked (by their ranks added up), then a ranked team against a team with at least 85% of its power rating (the save's power, season block +$50), then a conference game between the top two of the conference standings with a conference win each. At most eight.
- **Playoff picture** (`playoffPicture`): the format from the season flags (CFP 12, CFP 24 bit 2, CFP 4 bit 3, BCS 2 bit 4; none without the postseason bit). The game's seeds once set; before that, as the game picks the field: CFP 12 and 24 take the Power 4 conference champions and the best-ranked Group of 6 champion (each conference's leader standing in before the title games), CFP 12 also Notre Dame in the top 12, then the best-ranked teams; seeds by rank. CFP 4 and BCS 2 take the top teams. CFP 24 shows the eight byes and the last four in; all show the first four out.
- **Heisman watch** (`heismanWatch`): points from the season's stats: passing yards / 20, passing TD × 4, interceptions × −2, rushing and receiving yards / 10, their TDs × 6, plus 2 per team win. Top five.

## Stories

`news.ts` writes the front page's stories from the latest import (user, 2026-11-12, for more variety):

- **Results of the last week:** upsets of ranked teams, Top 25 games, routs, your games, conference titles, the champion; a new No. 1, the unbeaten teams, the playoff field; the national leaders.
- **Upsets by rating:** up to two wins by a team rated at least 8 points lower (team OVR, offense and defense averaged) that aren't already a ranked upset.
- **Streaks:** 5-0, 8-0, 10-0 and 12-0 starts; a losing streak of five or more ended; a winning streak of six or more snapped.
- **Milestones** (since the previous import, any gap): a player passes 2,000/3,000/4,000 passing yards, 20/30/40 TD passes, 1,000/1,500/2,000 rushing yards, 1,000/1,500 receiving yards, 15/20/25 rushing TDs, 10/15 sacks or 6/9 interceptions; at most four, your players first.
- **Players of the week** (only when the latest import is exactly one week after the one before, with none of that week's results in it): the game keeps season totals only, so the week's stats are the difference between the two imports. Offense: passing yards / 20 + TD × 4 − INT × 2 + rushing and receiving yards / 10 + TD × 6; defense: tackles + sacks × 3 + interceptions × 4. With a longer gap between imports, only the other stories. A player of the week's headline opens a window with his game (user, 2026-11-12: "I have no idea how Hubert 'wrecked' the game … open a modal that shows that player's stats from that game"): the result and his week's lines (passing comp/att/yds/TD/int, rushing att/yds/avg/TD/fum, receiving rec/yds/avg/TD, defense tackles/sacks/int; `Story.box`), with a link to his player page. Stories about a player (stat leaders, milestones) link to his page (`Story.player`; user: "these links about stat leaders should link to their player page not team page"); the rest to the team. The lead story and the three below it link the same way.
- **Rivalries** (`rivalries.ts`, 82 named pairs such as the Iron Bowl; `rivalryOf`): a story for each rivalry game of the week, ahead of ordinary results (your teams and ranked teams first).
- **Conference races** (from two weeks before the regular season's last week): the top four races, as clinched, shared lead or lead, from the conference standings.
- **Records broken** (from the second season): a stat's season leader (`RECORD_STATS`) passing the best of every earlier season, once (not again while he keeps adding to it); "past his own" when he already held it.
- **Commitments** (offseason): the latest recruiting week's 4★ and 5★ commitments, your teams' and flips, at most six.
- **Wording:** two or three phrasings per story type, picked by the story (the same story always reads the same).

## Season awards and the records book

**Season awards** (`awards.ts`, `AwardsPage.tsx`, `#dynasty/awards/<year>`; user, 2026-11-12: "Heisman, All-Conference and All-American teams, worked out from the stats"). Handed out once a season's last game is in. Every Active Roster player gets a score at his role: QB passing yards / 20 + TD × 4 − INT × 2 + rushing yards / 10 + rushing TD × 6; HB and FB rushing and receiving yards / 10 + TD × 6; WR and TE receiving yards / 10 + TD × 6 + catches / 4; offensive linemen (no stats) OVR + wins / 2; DE, DT and LB tackles + sacks × 3 + INT × 4; CB and S the same with INT × 5; K field goals × 3 − misses + extra points / 2; P yards a punt (10 punts or more). Stats and OVR together (user, 2026-11-12: "stats are very important in the decision-making, they aren't everything"; "they all should take overall into effect … all conference and all American teams … a little bit more stats based"): each player's stat score and OVR are ranked against everyone at his award spot (0 last to 1 first); only the leaders by stats are considered (top 10 for a position award, the top 4 per place, at least 6, for a team), and among them the best blend wins: 60% stats and 40% OVR for the Heisman and the position awards, 70/30 for the All-American and All-Conference teams (`AWARD_STATS_WEIGHT`, `TEAM_STATS_WEIGHT`). The Heisman blends the top 15 of the Heisman watch (stats and team wins) with their OVR rank. Each line shows his OVR. Teams: QB 1, RB 2, WR 3, TE 1, OL 5, DL 4, LB 3, CB 2, S 2, K 1, P 1. All-American first and second team (nobody on both), each conference's first team (independents left out), the Heisman (the next four are finalists) and the best quarterback, running back, receiver, offensive lineman, defensive player and kicker. The player page lists his **Honors**; History links to the awards.

**Records book** (`records.ts`, `RecordsPage.tsx`, `#dynasty/records[/<team>]`, linked from History; the single-season records moved there from History). The whole league or one school (a school's records count only what was done there): single-season and career top 10 for the `RECORD_STATS` stats, and team records (top 5): most wins and points in a season, fewest points allowed in a season (finished seasons only), most points in a game and the biggest wins.

## Names and skin tones

`appearance.ts` (user, 2026-11-12). Players are White or Black in the app; that decides the skin tone and, for generated players, which names fit together. Nothing of it goes into the ROM (the game has one skin tone); the tones are for the app's portraits.

- **Share by position** (`BLACK_SHARE`, the user's numbers): QB 45%, HB 95.5, FB 47, WR 81.1, TE 35.4, LT 48.8, LG 49.5, C 34.3, RG 47.9, RT 48.7, LE 75.9, RE 70, DT 73.5, LOLB 75.1, MLB 69.6, ROLB 73.4, CB 98.2, FS 89.6, SS 89.2, K 3.8, P 4.8.
- **Skin tones** (`SKIN_TONES`, `DPlayer.skin` 0-3): Light and Tan for White players, Brown and Dark for Black players, half and half.
- **Name odds** (`nameOdds.json`, built by `cli/nameodds.ts`): for every first and last name in the CFB 27 file, how likely it's a Black player's: the US Census 2010 surname table (public domain) and Tzioumis (2018) first names (Harvard Dataverse, CC0), as copied in the `surgeo` package on PyPI. Names they don't have (many modern first names, hyphenated and rare surnames) are estimated from the other half of the names and the positions of the CFB 27 players who have them, pulled toward the average when few do.
- **Starting players** (`assignBlack`): per position, the share of the position's players, chosen by how their names point plus random noise (a logistic draw), so names lean the choice without fixing it.
- **Generated players** (`makeRecruit`): White or Black by the position's share, then a first and a last name weighted by how often they appear among CFB 27 players and how well they fit (count × odds, or × 1 − odds); never a real CFB 27 player's full name. Only names the game can show well: first names up to 10 characters, last names up to 12 (`MAX_FIRST`, `MAX_LAST`), in the game's font (A-Z a-z 0-9 ' - . space).
- **In the ROM** (the season ROM's names set): every name goes through `gameText` (accents dropped, other characters out, at most 23 characters, the room Team Rosters has for a last name).

## Prestige

`src/core/dynasty/prestige.ts`. Every school has 1-6 stars per season (`DSeason.prestige`). Scale (user, 2026-11-12): final poll top 10 = 6, top 20 = 5, top 35 = 4, top 70 = 3, top 100 = 2, else 1; when the season ends a school moves at most one star toward the tier it finished in. Start: College Football 27's prestige at launch (0-5 in half stars, `CFB27_PRESTIGE`); schools are ranked by it (ties by team OVR in the ROM) and given the tier of their place, so the first season has 10 six-star, 10 five-star, 15 four-star, 35 three-star, 30 two-star and 38 one-star programs. Recruiting and the portal read prestige as (stars − 1) / 5.

## The offseason

`src/core/dynasty/offseason.ts`, pure steps that each return a new dynasty; seeded, so the same choices give the same offseason. `Dynasty.offseason` holds the step, the seed, the events (for the news and the next season's `events`) and the players as they were at the start (Start the offseason over).

1. **startOffseason** (needs every game played; the app has no way to end a season early, user 2026-11-12): SR+ leave; seniors go pro (chance (OVR − 80) / 14, up to 85%), else come back as SR+ (30%) or graduate; juniors go pro (chance (OVR − 84) / 12, up to 70%). Everyone else moves up a class. Development waits for the end of the offseason (user, 2026-11-12: training progression "should happen after recruiting (to everyone except for HS recruits) … just prior to the final depth chart phase. It's basically the end result of the offseason training"): it runs with the cuts (step 4, `trainPlayers`), for every returning player still on a team (transfers at their new school, with its training focus); new signees and walk-ons don't develop that year. So the projected depth chart, transfer decisions, recruiting needs and cuts all see last season's ratings. Development: gain = (potential − OVR) × rate (FR 0.45, SO 0.40, JR 0.35, SR 0.30) × random 0.5-1.5, +1 for one of the team's 32, ± noise. The gain goes to the ratings his position's OVR counts, as numbers, shared out by each rating's pace (user, 2026-11-12: "SPD shouldn't be able to progress as much as AWR"): Awareness 1.4, Hands/Blocking/Tackling/Accuracy 1.2, Power 1.0, Range 0.8, Quickness 0.7, Speed 0.5 (Offseason rules, Development), scaled so the OVR still moves by the gain; a rating that reaches 100 or its yearly cap passes the rest to the others, and what none can take is lost (`develop`, `paceOf`, `capOf`). **Yearly caps** (user, 2026-11-12: "+30 AWR? Doesn't that seem like A LOT? +13 SPD?" … "yeah cap them"): at most Awareness +18, Power/Hands/Blocking/Tackling/Accuracy +14, Quickness/Range +10, Speed +6 rating points a year (rules; training focus moves them like the paces). Claude first proposed +10/+7/+5/+3, but in the 30-season run those made the league sink (median team best 22 from 80 to 67, since OVR moves only about half as fast as the ratings and recruits start 7-13 below); the caps above are about the old top-10% year, so they only trim the outliers (one offseason: Awareness up to +54, Quickness and Accuracy up to +60, Speed up to +27), and the league stays at the 2026 level (median about 80, starters about 77, from 2031 to 2056). Caps are in rating points, so they work the same with one-point ratings (the fine-ratings test ROM). Bigger players also put on 0-6 lb; see "Ratings". **Training focus** (`training.ts`; user, 2026-11-12): on the Offseason page, before the offseason, each of your teams splits 100 points over Strength program (Power, Range), Speed development (Speed, Quickness), Technique (Hands, Blocking, Tackling, Accuracy) and Game knowledge (Awareness), at most 40 in one area (user: "maybe we max at 40 at ±20%"). 25 points = the normal paces; 40 makes an area's ratings grow 20% faster, 0 makes them 20% slower (straight lines in between; `trainingFactor`). The year's OVR gain doesn't change, so the focus shapes the player, not how good he gets (an HB's +4 year with Speed development at 40: SPD +5.4 instead of +4.9, AWR +12.2 instead of +13.6). The page doesn't say which ratings an area affects (user: "to keep a little mystery"); the Offseason rules do (`trainingMax`, `trainingEffect`). Computer teams train evenly (user: "cpu stays at 25 each for now"). Kept per team in `Dynasty.training` until changed. In a 30-season CPU-only run team strength settles as before (median best 22 about 83.5 from 2030 on); about 3,300 ratings sit at 100 by then, mostly upperclassmen's Awareness and Speed (was about 1,600 with even paces). Potential is hidden: given at the first offseason (OVR + 6-18 for a FR … 0-3 for an SR+) or by stars when signed.
2. **runPortal**: nobody leaves too soon for playing time (user, 2026-11-12: the game can't give a backup a few snaps). Active Roster players never enter; backups only after two seasons as backups: 10%, +10% when the starter ahead of him stays, +10% for a senior (`transferChance`). So the first offseason has no transfers. Teams that need the position take them, best players first, stronger programs first in line for the good ones, at most four per team. Nobody takes him: he leaves the dynasty.
3. **runSigningDay**: each team signs 64 − roster (at most 25). Position: the biggest need against the targets, two per slot in the game (QB 2, HB 4, FB 2, WR 8, TE 4, each OL spot 2, LE 2, RE 2, DT 4, LOLB 2, MLB 4, ROLB 2, CB 8, FS 4, SS 2, K 2, P 2 = 64). Then walk-ons for any position with fewer players than slots (`fillMinimums`). Stars by prestige (last season's rank, record, title, roster strength). A recruit is made from the league's own players of the position ("New players" under "Ratings"), with a new name and a US hometown from the starting players. Class points: 5★ 100, 4★ 60, 3★ 30, 2★ 12, 1★ 4.
4. **applyCuts** ("Make the cuts and train"): down to 64 when a team has more (rare: walk-ons for position minimums), lowest value first (OVR + half the room to potential + years left), never below one per slot. User teams choose (the suggestion is checked). Then walk-ons up to 64, where the need is biggest. Then the offseason's training (`trainPlayers`, step 1); the next step's page shows each user team's most improved players. **Training results** (`TrainingPage.tsx`, `trainingResults` and `trainingYears` in career.ts; user, 2026-11-12: "a Training Results page, roster ordered by most improved"): `#dynasty/training/<team>-<year>`, linked from that step ("All training results") and the team page. Any team and any past offseason: every returning player (last season's year record against this season's, or his current ratings while the offseason is at its last step), biggest OVR gain first, with each rating's change ("AWR +30", weight in pounds) and the school a transfer came from; new signees and walk-ons aren't listed.
5. **finishOffseason**: unique jersey numbers per team (the best player keeps his), the Active Roster and the backups per team (`depth.ts`: the best OVR at each spot), their game ratings (the steps of their own numbers) and team OVRs (from the starters' OVRs), and the next season.

**Full recruiting** (`src/core/dynasty/recruiting.ts`; chosen on the Offseason page instead of random classes, each offseason):
- **Who enters the portal** (`portalEntries`): the same rule as the random version (`transferChance`: backups after two seasons). They become prospects; their team can't easily get them back (fit × 0.3).
- **The pool** (`startRecruiting`): about 1.4 prospects per open spot in the country (spots = 64 − roster, at most 25), positions in proportion to the country's needs, stars from a national spread, made like the random classes' recruits; each prospect wants playing time, prestige and being close to home in random proportions (portal players mostly playing time).
- **A week** (`runRecruitingWeek`): every team spends 1,000 points, at most 60 on one prospect, and makes offers. Interest gained = points × (0.5 + fit) × (1.2 with an offer, else 0.6) × reach × noise. Fit = his priorities · (playing time he'd get by depth at his position, the school's prestige, closeness: same state 1, same region 0.6, else 0.25). Reach = 1 − 0.2 × (his stars − (prestige stars − 1)), 0.3-1.15. He commits when the leading school (that offered and has a spot) reaches the threshold for his stars (1★ 60 … 5★ 320; portal players 70% of it) and leads the second by 25%, and not before week 2 (3★), 3 (4★) or 4 (5★). A committed 4-5★ prospect can flip to a school two stars more prestigious that outbids his school by 50%. CPU teams target their needs within their star range (up to 2 + prestige × 4 stars), points to the best targets (score squared).
- **Scouting** (`scoutProspect`, `scoutLevel`; user, 2026-11-12: points "out of the main pool", OVR first, then all ratings, both allowed in one week; costs 10 and 20, the user's pick over 20/30 and 10/10). Your teams see a high-school prospect's stars, position, hometown, height and weight; scouting reveals his OVR (10 points), then the ratings his position uses (20 more). Points come out of the week's 1,000 at once (`scoutSpent`, reset each week) and don't count toward the 60 for one prospect. Revealed stays revealed (`scouted`, per user team). Portal players show everything. The prospect list sorts by stars, then known OVR, then name, so it doesn't give unscouted OVRs away. CPU teams don't scout.
- **Signing day** (`signingDay`): the uncommitted pick the school that offered with the most interest, a spot left and a need at his position; then teams with spots left take the best unsigned high-school players at positions they need (stronger programs first each round). Portal players nobody takes leave. Walk-ons for positions below one per slot.

**Career records** (`career.ts`; user, 2026-11-12: "save a player's yearly progression/ratings every year. That way we can add later a way to display the progression over their career"). Every player on a team gets a record per season (`DPlayer.years`: year, team, position, class, OVR and the ten ratings in `RATINGS` order, one decimal so the progress between steps stays): at the dynasty's start, when each offseason sets up the next season, and again when that season's roster is edited before it's played (editor, CSV, lineup moves). Players who leave keep their records. About 0.9 MB of dynasty file per season. Shown on the player page as the **progression chart** (`Progression` in pages.tsx, from his second season on): a small line per rating and one for OVR, 0-100, a point per season, in the team's color.

**Career stats** (user, 2026-11-12: "show the previous school(s) if they transferred"; "Do we have career stats? Do they appear after one year of stats below the ratings?"). The player page shows this season's stats at the top ("2026 season at Texas State") and, below the ratings, a **Career stats** table per category (`CareerStats` in pages.tsx): a row per season, oldest first, with the school he played for and his class that season (from his year records), and a Career row (sums; Long is the longest) once there are two seasons. It shows once he has stats from a season before this one (or from two seasons); on phones the tables scroll sideways. The header says "Transferred from Texas State (2027)" for each transfer.

**Player page header** (user, 2026-11-12: "make the jersey number bigger, more prominent. Also, i think the team logo should go in the header too"; "i like how LSU roster puts the player number in yellow before the name"): the number before the name at the name's size, in the school's second color when it stands out from the first (else the header's text color; `accentOn` in ui.tsx), then a thin bar; the team's logo at the right of the header (96 px, 56 px on phones), linking to the team page.

**Experience** (`experienceOf`; user, 2026-11-12: "an indicator of their 'Experience' as letters earned at the current school next to their class. So, 1L, 2L, 3L, 4L, TR … Maybe as 'TR (Louisville)'"). Next to the class on the player page: letters at his current school, one per completed season on that team ("2L"); "TR (Louisville)" for a transfer who hasn't played a season there yet (after that his letters, and the header keeps "Transferred from …"); "HS" for a first-year player out of high school (Claude's choice for the case the user's list didn't cover). Tap or hover explains it. The starting players' earlier seasons count by their 2026 class (FR 0, SO 1, JR 2, SR 3, SR+ 4): CFB 27 has no history, so they're taken as having been at their school all along.

**Walk-ons and stars** (user, 2026-11-12). Walk-ons are modeled on the bottom 8% of the league's players at the position, 7-13 OVR younger, potential 55-70 (about 58 OVR to start, like a 1★ recruit; starters average about 75). **Diamonds in the rough** ("a few rare ones would be cool"): 3% of walk-ons (`DIAMOND_CHANCE`) get a top recruit's potential, 84-96 (`DIAMOND_POT`); they start as weak as any walk-on and grow fast (development is a share of the room left), about 18 a year in the first offseason's 573 walk-ons. Every signed player keeps his recruiting stars and class year (`DPlayer.recruit`; 0 = walk-on), shown on the player page ("★★★ recruit (2027)", "Walk-on (2027)"); the starting 2026 players have none (CFB 27 has no recruiting stars). Class rankings show "22 signed, **13** × 5★, **7** × 4★" (user: "need some distinction between the numbers").

**Cuts** (`suggestCuts`, `cutOrder`, `keepValue`; user, 2026-11-12: "I'd rather not cut a 75 OVR FR when i have a 76 OVR senior that i could cut … The CPU teams need to be smart like this too"). The same logic for CPU teams and your suggestion: next season's Active Roster (the depth chart the season would start with) stays; backups aren't in the game, so they're judged by their expected OVR the next two seasons (development by class and potential; JR one season; SR and SR+ none, so they go first); the lowest go first across all positions. Minimums (`GROUP_MIN`, user: "do we really need to require the user have 8 CBs? Maybe we set min per position"): every Active Roster spot plus a backup per group: QB 2, HB/FB 4, WR 5, TE 3, OL 7, DL 5, LB 5, CB 5, S 4, K/P 2 (42 of the 64); the rest is the team's choice. Your own cuts below a minimum are refused; walk-ons fill a group below its minimum after signing day.

**Offseason steps** (user, 2026-11-12): 1. Depth chart (projected, editable for your teams; user: "a step for projected depth chart prior to players leaving. That way we can move a HB2 to HB1 if we really want to ensure he doesn't transfer"), 2. Players leaving (graduates, early entries, players entering the transfer portal; user: "just a view of who graduated, who went pro, and who is transferring"), 3. Transfer portal (random classes) or Recruiting, 4. Signing day and cuts, 5. Next season: then the final depth chart on the team page (the Pos menus, before the first import) and the ROM. Before signing day rosters are thin, so the projected chart may show a player in two spots (the depth chart's fallbacks).

**Transfers** (`transferChance`, `portalContext`, `chooseTransfers`; user, 2026-11-12: "The third or fourth WR on a depth chart should try and find a starting job elsewhere, if available"; "they should have lower chances of transferring if they actually get playing time … it rewards the user to distribute the ball"). Decided after the projected depth chart, for every team: by his role last season, starters 1%, the Active Roster's depth spots (HB2, the 3rd and 4th WRs, TE2, DT2, MLB2, the 3rd and 4th CBs, FS2; `DEPTH_SLOTS`) 8%, backups 12%; +10% when next season's (projected) depth chart has him out of a starting spot; +5% seniors; then up to 80% less with playing time (his carries and catches, or tackles, sacks and interceptions, against half of what a typical starter at his position got; linemen and backups have no stats). Players signed this offseason can't. Random classes: he moves only to a team where he'd start (beats its weakest projected starter at his position; better programs first, at most 4 a team), else he stays. Full recruiting: portal players are prospects; nobody signs him, or his old school does, and he stays. About 700 enter and 450 move in the first offseason. The 1994 edition will have no portal (user), once it supports dynasties.

**Top schools and pitches** (user, 2026-11-12: "every recruit should start with a list of his interested/top schools. **Offseason rules** (`rules.ts`, `RulesPage.tsx`; user, 2026-11-12: "give users total control over how cpu offseason logic works … adjust nearly every threshold and percentage chance … this will also help me understand how to debug and refine the logic"; "an export offseason settings option so the user can import those settings into another dynasty"). **Pitches** (user, 2026-11-12: "playing time, program prestige, proximity to home, academics, and championship contender. That's five"). What a prospect cares about is a random draw per pitch times the rules' weights by stars, scaled to add up to 1; a school's fit = the sum of each pitch's weight × the school's score: playing time (his place on its depth chart), prestige (stars), closeness (same state 1, region 0.6, else 0.25), academics (College Football 27's Academic Prestige grade for all 138 schools, `academics.ts`, read from a public CFB 27 team grades table on 2026-11-12; A+ = 1 … F = 0.1), Championship Contender (`contenderScores`: last season's final poll place, top 4 = 1 down to 0 at 64th, weighted 50% by default, and the roster as it stands, its best 22 OVRs with commitments and without portal players, ranked against the country; user: "look at last year's results and the current roster so far, eg. a top returning class means the team has a shot"). **Pitching** (user, 2026-11-12: "I don't think the interest need to be shown … only shown to be a match … you get rewarded if your school matches what the player is interested in"). What a prospect wants is hidden. On your board you choose a pitch per prospect each week (optional; it stays until you change it); after the week he reacts, shown as a chip and in the pitch menu: + his interest (one of his top 2 pitches) and your school strong there (score ≥ 0.65): points ×1.5; ~ his interest, in between: ×1.15; − his interest but your school weak (< 0.4): ×0.7, less than no pitch; ✗ not his interest: ×1. School scores 0-1: playing time (his place on your depth chart), prestige, closeness, academics, contender (`pitchScores`, `pitchReaction`, `interestsOf`; reactions per user team in `RecruitingState.reactions`). Computer teams learn a prospect's interests the way you do (user, 2026-11-12, on prestige-based guessing: "is this fair? does it lean too heavy towards schools remaining powerhouses forever?"): a + pitch is kept, untried pitches come before a ~, − and ✗ are never repeated (`learnedPitch`; reactions are kept for every team). All other numbers are rules (Pitches). With the computer pitching, commitments in five weeks rose to about 620 (from 405) in the test offseason.

A 10-season check (a harness, not in the repo: each season's poll = team strength, its best 22 OVRs, plus luck; full recruiting every offseason): of the 10 starting 6-star programs, 5 were still 6-star after 10 seasons with prestige-based guessing and 3 with learning; 6-star programs signed 11-16% of the 4-5★ recruits with guessing and 9-15% with learning (they are 7% of the schools). The median team strength rose from about 80 to 83 over the 10 seasons (development outpacing departures), to watch.
